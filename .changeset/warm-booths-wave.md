Polish in the office and the factory. An idle foreman up in its booth now slacks where you can see it: binoculars on the line, a brew, a call, a lean back. A PR's crate goes from just where the robot arm sets it down, down the chute and onto the hallway's belt. A merged PR's frame goes up on the wall as its lead gets back to the desk with the reply. Regulars wear more than glasses (a flower, earrings, freckles, a pin, a plaster), and no two on the floor share a name. The kitchen's mug wall hangs clear of the coffee machine and its steam, a fluorescent tube in every office room flickers now and then, and every factory press has a pilot lamp.
